Abstract

Coordinated multi-robot navigation in unstructured environments often requires maintaining formations, but rigid formations in constrained spaces necessitate adaptive formation control. Diffusion models generate smooth navigation trajectories through probabilistic denoising, but rely on expert demonstrations that are difficult to obtain for multi-robot teams. Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) avoids demonstrations by learning through environment interaction, but its discrete decision-making often produces jerky motions. In multi-robot settings, centralized diffusion with online RL becomes intractable as the joint action space grows exponentially with team size. To overcome these limitations, we propose Reinforced Diffusion on Graphs (ReDiG), one of the first principled multi-robot learning approaches that enables fully decentralized coordinated navigation with smooth formation adaptation. ReDiG uses a graph network to aggregate neighbor information via message passing and produce a team-level representation that conditions each robot's diffusion policy for team-aware, smooth trajectory generation. ReDiG further formulates graph-conditioned diffusion policy refinement as an online actor-critic RL problem to adapt diffusion policies under dynamic teaming and environmental feedback, while using a spring-damper reward to encourage synchronized formation adaptation. ReDiG is trained in an end-to-end unified manner, with the diffusion and critic gradients jointly backpropagated through their networks and the graph network. Extensive experiments with both simulated and physical robot teams demonstrate that ReDiG achieves state-of-the-art coordinated navigation while enabling smooth and adaptive formation control.
